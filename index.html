Strategy Good Advice

USE REFERRALS/NETWORKING/RELATIONSHIPS

you can do your own research here, but everything I've seen leans HEAVILY towards referrals leading to getting hired. Is it guaranteed? No, but using referrals or connections you've made can be a huge advantage and should be prioritized above everything else. Anecdotally, I've used a strong relationship from my past work to get an offer after just one interview. I could've literally handed them the worst version of my resume.

Personal Projects

If you have the free time, work on personal projects that align with a role you're interviewing for. This not only helps you prepare for the interview, it gives you something to talk about during the interview (maybe), and is something you can add to your portfolio.

Portfolio Site

Have a portfolio site - just somewhere where you can dump everything about you and any personal projects that you might want to show off. I can't tell you if this really helped my chances or not, but man did it feel good to put together and have a place I could showcase my work. It's definitely something I've proud of and has been useful in some cases where people wanted links to my podcasts, blog posts, etc and I had them all referenced and linked. Some applications even asked for "website" links, I just put my portfolio site there.

  • Use github pages for this. It's free and functions just like a website. Perfect use case imo, you don't have to pay for a domain or host anything yourself.

Thank You Letters

Some people swear by thank you letters. I've personally done it once and didn't get the job, but it may help in a lot of cases. The one time I did it, it was recommended by the company's internal recruiter, so I assume that was pretty solid advice in that case. I'm not sure it will make a bad interview look good, but it may help give you that smallest little nudge to get you from second place to first. This is a low risk, high reward kind of thing, I don't think it could ever really hurt your chances.

Interview Prep Good Advice

SUPER underrated video

Watch on YouTube
  • a big reason for having interviews in the first place is for the employer to evaluate risk. What's the risk they bring to themselves or their organization by hiring you? Risk to their reputation or something else.
  • Use the STAR method to talk about examples from your past. During my time job hunting, I literally just went through projects or situations I was a part of and wrote out the whole thing in STAR. Having a bunch of examples to have on hand is helpful for when interviewers start with the "Tell me about a time…". I have a really hard time being put on the spot to remember anything I've done in the past. Like I literally forget every impactful contribution I've ever made. Having real examples written down ahead of time helped me with this big time.

Some specific examples that I've gotten from interviews:

What we're looking for

Behavioral interviews can explore a range of skills including—but not limited to—communication, influencing others, collaboration or driving a project from start to finish.We highly recommend using the STAR method to structure your answer. We want you to be able to share a clear, detailed story about the process of making a decision, completing a task or overcoming an obstacle. Pull it all together by telling us about the results of your actions and what you learned from the experience.

Remember, your real experiences make the most powerful answers. We prefer that you talk about actual examples rather than hypothetical situations. And if you're not immediately sure how to answer a question, pause—don't panic. It's perfectly acceptable to ask your interviewer for a moment to gather your thoughts before diving in.

Tell me about a time when...

  • You used persuasion to successfully convince someone to see things your way.
  • You used good judgment and logic when solving a problem.
  • You had to go above and beyond the call of duty to get a job done.
  • You had too many things to do and you were required to prioritize your tasks.
  • You anticipated potential problems and developed preventive measures.

Resume Tips

Summary

Some people like having a summary, others say it doesn't help. Up to you if you want to use it. I added one late in my job search and don't have good data on whether or not it really helped me

File Format

Also up in the air if employers prefer docx or PDF - another thing I see mixed opinions online about.

Write Resume using Latex

Basically an easy way to write your resume "as code" that is easily converted to PDF or other formats. I would have claude handle all of this. It would write latex code and then could convert to PDF or docx. I would sometimes write it myself too and, if you know code, this is pretty easy to do as well. Better than trying to start with docx or editing a pdf directly.

  • Quick heads up that sometimes, my resumes would get flagged (only by google services) as having a virus. I could usually solve this by opening the PDF in a browser like Chrome and then print, save as PDF. I think this is just a result of the thing starting as code, but I'm not sure. Only some were flagged too, so your mileage may vary.
  • peerlist.io — What is a LaTeX Resume
  • Overleaf CV Gallery

Tailor for Each Role

Make sure your resume is tailored for each role you apply to. Jason Blanchard details making a "catch all" resume that contains ALL of your experience. Use that as your starting point and remove bullet points that don't help based on the job description.

  • I did something similar, but made a "master reference" that included all of my bullet points and then used claude to help me pull relevant ones into a new resume. Be sure to use your own best judgement here as well, sometimes I was very specific about which bullets I wanted to include and the reference sheet helped me keep track of them all.

Cover Letters

good if you feel the need to write them, but a good summary may cover the same ground in a better format (better chance of an employer reading a small, impactful section than a longer, more wordy cover letter). Again, mileage may vary here and what works with one employer may not work with another. There were some jobs I applied to that I felt really compelled to write a cover letter for. Mostly if I felt that it would be beneficial to explain how I fit the role. I'm not sure I'm the best to listen to here since I don't have any good evidence that any of my cover letters helped.
